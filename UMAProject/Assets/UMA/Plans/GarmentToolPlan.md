# UMA Garment Tool implementation plan

Add **Garment** to the Vertex Editor so an artist can draw a sewing pattern, fit it to a generated UMA character, and equip the result through a wardrobe recipe. Give the fitter a second entry point for an existing clothing slot. Deliver both as an optional editor plugin. The published clothing should work in an ordinary UMA player build.

The first garment should be a two-panel sleeveless vest. Its shoulder and side seams exercise sewing, its neck and armholes must stay open, and lifting the arms exposes poor weight transfer. A loose hem makes it easy to see whether the fitter preserves the silhouette. Use that garment to prove the entire workflow before expanding the pattern library.

## What the artist does

1. **Choose the body.** Open Garment on a built scene avatar. Select the body slots that provide fit surfaces, choose the reference pose, and mark or adjust shoulder, chest and waist landmarks. The stage shows the measured chest circumference and shoulder-to-hem length. Keep the measurement locations with the fit so a second artist can reproduce it.
2. **Draft the pattern.** Open a dockable 2D pattern canvas linked to the stage. Start blank or open a saved pattern. Draw closed panels with straight or Bézier edges, split an edge, enter lengths in centimetres, and mirror a piece. For the vest, draft a front and a back, make the front neckline deeper, and set the extra chest circumference desired for a loose fit. This extra circumference is garment *ease*. The small gap used to keep cloth clear of skin is a separate fit control.
3. **Sew and arrange.** Pair the shoulders and sides. Both views highlight the selected seam, its direction and the lengths of its two edges. Leave the neck, armholes and hem open. Place the front and back on the corresponding body landmarks, facing out. A reversed seam should be visible and reversible before fitting.
4. **Fit and inspect.** Pin the shoulders, run a coarse drape, then refine it. Pull or release a patch, change clearance locally, or return to the pattern to widen the chest. Show body intersections, seam gaps and stretched regions directly on the garment. For an existing slot, enter here through **Fit Existing Slot**, capture its reference fit and refit it after changing the body. Preserve its UVs, vertex order, weights and blendshape correspondence.
5. **Publish and try on.** Save the reusable pattern and the target-specific fit independently. Choose the UMA material, overlay, race, wardrobe category and output location. Bake the garment, equip the recipe through the preview avatar's normal wardrobe flow, and inspect it in pose. A second body can use the same pattern with its own measurements, arrangement and bake.

## Authoring data and regeneration

| Proposed record | What to persist |
| --- | --- |
| Garment pattern | Versioned panel outlines in physical units, stable point and edge IDs, mirror relationships, named dimensions, directed seam spans and intentional openings. Keep the editable flat pattern independent of the avatar and triangulation. |
| Garment fit | Pattern reference or existing slot reference, race, ordered body slots, reference DNA and pose, landmarks, panel placement, ease overrides, collision clearance, pins and local fit edits. Anchor pattern edits and pins in panel coordinates. Bind existing-slot edits to slot identity and topology. |
| Generated output | Standard UMA slot and wardrobe assets. Keep source and output references and their bake revision in the authoring data. A player loading the recipe needs only UMA and the baked assets. |

Give a pattern dimension either a fixed length or a measurement with an allowance. For example, a 96 cm chest with 8 cm of ease gives the vest a finished chest circumference of 104 cm. For equal front and back panels drawn as mirrored halves, each half is 26 cm wide at the chest line. On a 108 cm chest the same pattern evaluates to 29 cm per half. The neckline depth can keep its own fixed measurement. Show the evaluated dimensions before fitting so the artist can distinguish a sizing error from a drape problem.

Changing a curve or mesh density regenerates the mesh from the pattern. Resample pins and local edits from their panel coordinates and identify edits whose panel was removed. Invalidate body bindings when either topology changes. Treat downstream edits made directly to a baked slot as an independently edited result. Offer a new output or an explicit update of the linked assets, preserving their GUIDs and reporting which edits the update would replace.

## How the fit becomes a wearable

**Panel mesh and seams.** Validate closed, simple outlines before triangulation. Sample each paired span by arc length in its chosen direction and assign corresponding boundary samples on both panels. Constrained triangulation must retain those samples, open boundaries and panel winding. Use the flat panels as the source for padded UV islands in the garment's texture space. At a sewn seam, share the physical vertex and its skin weights while keeping the render copies needed for the two UV islands. Report ambiguous pairings and excessive length mismatch on the offending edges. Deliberate gathers and darts can follow once ordinary seams are reliable.

**Drape and clearance.** Start with a fixed-step CPU position-based solver in the editor. Use the flat panel lengths as rest lengths, bring paired seam samples together progressively, then relax stretch and bending under gravity, pins and collision against the selected body triangles. Preserve the last valid preview when a solve fails or is cancelled. Keep weight-donor selection separate from collision selection. A locked torso can support the cloth without becoming editable, and an adjacent arm should not supply weights to the side of the vest. Ship the first fit for a single clothing layer. Track unresolved self-intersections explicitly.

**Skinning and DNA.** Reuse the Scene Mesh Slot Builder's closest-triangle weight interpolation, bone remapping and conversion into slot bind space. Share the relevant routines behind its existing window. Allow garment panel or region restrictions and show unmapped or ambiguous vertices before bake. For a reusable slot, establish the base bake on the race's reference shape. Check it on the requested DNA variants using the equipped recipe. Bone-driven changes follow skinning. Body blendshapes that move the skin independently may need a corresponding clothing shape, which can be authored through the conformer and tested at matching weights. A successful fit on one preview does not establish a supported DNA range. [Scene Mesh Slot Builder](../Core/Editor/Scripts/SceneMeshSlotBuilderWindow.cs) · [Clothing Conformer](../Docs/ClothingConformer.md)

**Refit an existing slot.** Bind the garment to its source body before changing the body. Reuse the conformer's triangle mappings, signed offsets, welded-seam handling, topology checks and save-as-slot or blendshape operations. For loose regions, let the artist restrict donor regions, pin attachment points or relax around them. Highlight vertices beyond binding distance for correction. A different body topology requires fresh landmarks and a new fit. On every preview or bake that recalculates normals, use `UMABuddyNormals` so coincident UV and slot-edge battle buddies share one normal. Route around the Scene Mesh Slot Builder's existing missing-normals fallback when extracting its reusable code. [Clothing binding data](../Core/Scripts/ClothingConformer/ClothingBindData.cs) · [UMA buddy normals](../Core/StandardAssets/UMA/Scripts/UMABuddyNormals.cs)

**Stage and publishing.** The stage and its linked Mesh Modifier window currently select among four built-in workflows. Add a small editor-side extension point for Garment activation, scene input, preview rebuilds, unsaved work and cleanup. The plugin owns the canvas and temporary garment mesh. Keep drafts out of the stage's baked body mesh and its index-based sculpt adjustments. Integrate Save, Discard and Cancel with the existing leave-stage dialog. For publishing, share the Scene Mesh Slot Builder's slot validation, overlay, recipe and library registration logic. Validate in memory first and roll back partial asset or index changes if publishing fails. [Vertex Editor stage](../Core/Editor/StageUtils/VertexEditorStage.cs) · [Mesh Modifier editor](../Core/Editor/Scripts/MeshModifierEditor.cs) · [Stage saving](../Core/Editor/StageUtils/VertexEditorStage.Saving.cs)

## Delivery order and acceptance

| Milestone | Demonstration that closes it |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Prove the vest end to end | Use a programmatically defined front and back, shoulder pins and single material. Sew, drape, bake and equip the vest on a fresh avatar. Rebuild twice, raise the arms and inspect the loose hem and open armholes. Record the preview-to-equipped surface error in the reference pose. Establish acceptable seam, penetration and timing tolerances on this fixture. |
| 2. Put pattern editing in the stage | Recreate the vest from a blank canvas. Mirror, split, reverse a seam, undo, save, reopen and change the neckline. A bad outline identifies the faulty panel and keeps the previous preview. Changing preview resolution keeps valid seams and pins attached. |
| 3. Deliver both fitting paths | Fit the vest on two DNA shapes of one race. Refit an existing slot from its bound source shape and save a copy or corrective blendshape. Inspect underarms and waist in neutral, arms-raised and bent poses. Check for wrong-limb weights, penetration, seam separation and accidental closure of openings. Verify UVs, normals, skin weights and blendshape behavior after equip and rebuild. |
| 4. Deliver the optional plugin | Exercise Undo, stage exit, assembly reload, cancelled bake and explicit rebake. Test a core-only install and a main-plugin-only install. List **Garment Tool Examples** and **Garment Tool Tests** directly beneath **Garment Tool**, indented, in the installer. Package each separately. Put the original vest pattern and walkthrough in Examples and the automated fixtures in Tests. Verify a player can equip a baked recipe with the authoring plugin absent. |

Planning baseline is the current `C:/GitHub/UMA/UMAProject` checkout, Unity **6000.3.26f1** and installed UMA **3.1.6**. Run the implementation checks on the current checkout and record its actual versions, the fixture race, DNA values, pose, mesh density and measured errors with the results. [Unity project version](../../../ProjectSettings/ProjectVersion.txt) · [Installed UMA settings](../InternalDataStore/InGame/Resources/UMASettings.asset)

After the vest, add a short-sleeve shirt to test a sleeve cap sewn to an armhole, then a skirt to test a waistband supporting fabric far from either leg. Use those fixtures to decide when to add multi-edge seams, deliberate gathering, additional race profiles and self-collision for layered garments. Texture details such as topstitching can use the baked panel UVs in the optional Overlay Painter.

## Reference workflows

- Drawing flat pieces, pairing directed seams and arranging them around an avatar are established garment-authoring operations. Use these operations with UMA stage selection and an original pattern canvas. [Garment Studio](https://superhivemarket.com/products/garment-studio) · [Garment Tool](https://bartoszstyperek.gumroad.com/l/GarmentTool) · [Marvelous Designer manual](https://www.marvelousdesigner.com/manuals)
- Pins and cloth controls offer a direct way to tune a fit. Body measurements and painted corrections give artists ways to control garment shape locally. [Simply Cloth](https://superhivemarket.com/products/simply-cloth) · [Divine Cut](https://superhivemarket.com/products/divine-cut-smart-cloth-generator)
- A reusable template gives a first-time artist a garment they can take apart, adjust and fit again. The example vest should be an original UMA pattern built with the same tools available to the artist. [Cloth Weaver](https://clothweaver.com/)
